What is a gene?

A gene is a segment of DNA that contains instructions used by cells. Different versions of a gene are called alleles. Many human traits are influenced by multiple genes and environmental factors.

Chromosomes

Chromosomes are structures made of DNA and proteins. Most human body cells have 23 pairs of chromosomes. Eggs and sperm usually carry one chromosome from each pair.

Inheritance

Children inherit genetic information from both biological parents. A simple Punnett square can illustrate possible allele combinations for a single-gene model, but real inheritance can be more complex.

Worked example: Aa × Aa

Suppose both parents carry one dominant allele (A) and one recessive allele (a).

  1. Parent 1 can pass on A or a, each with a 1 in 2 chance.
  2. Parent 2 can also pass on A or a, each with a 1 in 2 chance.
  3. That gives 4 equally likely boxes: AA, Aa, Aa, aa.
  4. So the model gives AA 25%, Aa 50%, aa 25%. If A is dominant, 3 of 4 boxes (75%) show the dominant trait and 1 of 4 (25%) shows the recessive trait — a 3:1 ratio.

Try it above: select Aa and Aa, then press calculate to check the answer yourself. These are theoretical proportions for one simplified gene, not a prediction for any real family.

Genetics and human health

Some genetic variants can affect the chance of developing particular conditions. Other conditions involve many genes, lifestyle, and environmental influences. Genetic test results need qualified interpretation in their clinical context.

Important: This website is for general education only. It does not diagnose conditions, estimate personal risk, or replace a healthcare professional.
